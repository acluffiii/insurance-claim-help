Texas-first claim guides

Plain-English help when hail, or another loss, turns into an insurance claim

Most people do not spend much time with their policy until something is already damaged. These guides explain, in ordinary language, how common auto and home claims work — starting with hail — and point you to official sources so you can check the facts yourself.

This site is educational only. It is not legal advice or insurance advice, and it cannot tell you what your insurer will pay.

Hail claims

Auto and home hail questions, beginning with coverage and deductibles.

  • Is hail damage covered by comprehensive, and how does the deductible work?

    The most common mix-up: “comprehensive” is an auto coverage. Home policies use wind and hail coverage, often with a different deductible.

  • Will filing a hail claim raise my rates or get my policy nonrenewed?

    Texas rules protect weather claims like hail from home surcharges and from nonrenewal, but premiums can still change for other reasons.

  • Why roof hail claims get denied, and what official Texas help looks like

    Why a roof claim gets turned down, what the insurer must tell you in writing, and Texas’s official next steps.

  • Replacement cost vs. actual cash value on a roof hail claim

    Why a roof claim often pays in two checks, what depreciation is, and what an ACV roof means for your out-of-pocket cost.

  • Should I file if the hail damage is close to my deductible?

    The deductible math, what can and can't count against you in Texas, and why a quick look can miss hail damage.

  • Storm-chaser contractors and deductible waivers after a hail storm

    Why a contractor can't legally waive or rebate your Texas home deductible, and TDI's warning signs after a storm.
