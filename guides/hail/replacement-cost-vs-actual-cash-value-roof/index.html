Replacement cost vs. actual cash value on a roof hail claim

Published 2026-11-20

A common surprise after a roof hail claim: the insurer "approved a new roof," but the first check is thousands of dollars short of the roofer's price. Sometimes that's normal and the rest is coming later. Sometimes it's all you'll get. The difference is whether your roof is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value.

The short version: replacement cost coverage pays to repair or replace the roof at today's prices, minus your deductible, but usually in two checks: the second one comes after you do the work. Actual cash value coverage subtracts depreciation for the roof's age and wear, and that money doesn't come back. Your declarations page and roof endorsement tell you which one you have.

The two kinds of coverage, in plain English

  • Replacement cost value (RCV). The NAIC explains that with RCV coverage, "your policy will pay the cost to repair or replace your damaged property without deducting for depreciation."
  • Actual cash value (ACV). With ACV coverage, the NAIC says, "your policy will pay the depreciated cost to repair or replace your damaged property."
  • Depreciation is what gets subtracted. TDI's home insurance guide defines it as "a decrease in value because of wear and age." The NAIC says insurers usually calculate it "based on the condition of the property when it was lost or damaged, what a new item would cost, and how long the item would normally last."

TDI's own example: a 10-year-old roof

TDI's home insurance guide walks through one roof both ways. These are TDI's illustration numbers, not real prices:

| | Replacement cost policy | Actual cash value policy | |---|---|---| | Cost of a new roof today | $10,000 | $10,000 | | Value after depreciation | — | $7,000 (TDI's example) | | Deductible | $2,000 | $2,000 | | Insurer pays | $8,000 | $5,000 | | You pay | $2,000 | $5,000 |

The NAIC's version uses $15,000 of roof damage and a $1,000 deductible. In its example, the RCV family is paid $14,000 and the ACV family, after $10,000 of depreciation, is paid $4,000. Same storm, same roof damage, very different checks.

Why replacement cost still arrives in two checks

This is the part that trips up a lot of people with good coverage. TDI's home guide says that with a replacement cost policy, "most companies pay with two checks":

1. First check: "for the estimated cost of repairs, minus depreciation and your deductible." In other words, the first check looks like an ACV payment. 2. Second check: "for the amount it kept for depreciation after it gets the bill for the finished job." This held-back amount is often called recoverable depreciation.

TDI's disaster-claims FAQ answers the exact question people ask, "I have replacement cost coverage on my house. So why am I only getting a check for actual cash value?" Its answer: "Once you’ve started repairs and show proof to your company, they’ll send you the remainder to equal the replacement cost."

Two things to pin down early:

  • Your repair deadline. TDI's guide says "You usually must complete repairs within a certain period of time. Ask your agent or adjuster if you’re not sure how long you have." If you never replace the roof, or miss that window, you may not get the depreciation back.
  • What proof they want. Usually the final invoice. Ask the adjuster in writing what they need to release the second check.

Older roofs and "ACV roof" endorsements

You can have replacement cost on the house and still have actual cash value on the roof. TDI's roof tip warns: "As roofs age, some companies will switch to actual cash value. And if your roof is in poor condition, your company might not cover your roof at all. Your company should tell you when they change your coverage."

Before a storm, or right after one, check:

  • Your declarations page and any roof endorsement listed on it. Our guide to hail coverage and deductibles shows how to find the deductible piece.
  • Your renewal paperwork. TDI's homeowners bill of rights says that if a company wants to make certain "material changes" instead of nonrenewing, it must explain them in writing at least 30 days before the renewal date.
  • Whether your wind and hail deductible is different. The NAIC and TDI both suggest asking. A percentage wind/hail deductible on top of depreciation can leave an older roof with a very small check, or none.

TDI is also blunt about one thing: "your insurance company won’t pay for a new roof just because it’s old or worn out." ACV doesn't change what's covered (hail damage), only how much is paid for it.

If you think the depreciation is too high

Depreciation is a number, so it can be questioned. Ask the adjuster for the estimate line by line, including the depreciation applied to each item and how the roof's age and condition were judged. If you have records showing the roof is newer than they assumed (a permit, an invoice, a warranty), send them.

If you still disagree about the amount, TDI's "isn't paying enough" tip explains the policy's appraisal process: you and the company each hire an appraiser, the two pick an umpire, and the result is binding. You pay your own appraiser and half the umpire's costs, and your policy may have a deadline to ask for it. Our guide on roof hail claim denials covers appraisal, TDI complaints, and the 61-day notice before a lawsuit in more detail.

Mortgage company on the check

If you have a mortgage, TDI's home guide says "the insurance company will make the check for repairs out to both you and your mortgage company." The lender may ask for the work list, estimates, who's doing the work, and a timeline. TDI's homeowners bill of rights says the lender must tell you what it needs within 10 days of getting the check, and send the money or tell you how to get it released within 10 days after you ask. TDI's guide says to complain to the Texas Attorney General's Office (800-252-8011) if the mortgage company doesn't release the money on time.

Before you sign or cash anything

TDI's disaster-claims FAQ says the first check "might also only be a partial payment to help you start repairs," and warns: "Talk to your company before you sign the check. Some companies consider your claim final and settled if you sign the check." Ask whether the check is a final settlement or the first of two.

Quick checklist

  • Find out whether the roof is RCV or ACV (declarations page, roof endorsement, renewal notices).
  • Get the adjuster's estimate showing replacement cost, depreciation, and deductible on separate lines.
  • If RCV: ask how long you have to finish repairs and what proof releases the depreciation.
  • If ACV: plan for the depreciation as out-of-pocket; question it with records if the roof's age or condition is wrong.
  • If a mortgage company is on the check, ask early what it needs to release funds.
