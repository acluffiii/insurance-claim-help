Hail claims

Will filing a hail claim raise my rates or get my policy nonrenewed?

Published 2026-11-18

After a hail storm, a lot of people hesitate to file because they have heard that "any claim" will raise their premium or get them dropped. In Texas, the official answer is more specific than that, and it is different for a house than for a car.

The short version: Texas rules say a home insurer can't charge you more, or refuse to renew your policy, because of a claim for weather damage such as hail. An auto insurer can't refuse to renew your policy because of a weather-damage claim. That does not mean your premium can never go up — it can change for reasons that have nothing to do with your claim.

What TDI says about home policies

The Texas Department of Insurance tip Will my premium go up if I file a claim? starts with "It depends on the type of claim and how many you file." It then lists what home companies can't charge you more for, including "Claims for damage from natural causes, including weather."

Nonrenewal works the same way. TDI's home insurance guide says a company may nonrenew your policy if "You file three or more nonweather-related claims in three years," and that "A company can raise your rates if you file two or more nonweather-related claims." The key word in both rules is nonweather. TDI's nonrenewal tip gives examples of nonweather events: "fires, rodent damage, or broken pipes."

The rule behind this is Texas Insurance Code Section 551.107. For homeowners, standard fire, and farm and ranch owners policies, it says a "claim" for these purposes does not include a claim "resulting from a loss caused by natural causes," or a claim "that is filed but is not paid or payable under the policy." It allows a premium surcharge after two or more counted claims in the preceding three policy years, and allows nonrenewal after three or more counted claims in a three-year period, but only if the company sent the required warning after the second one.

What TDI says about auto policies

For cars, TDI's auto insurance guide says a company "can't nonrenew your policy because you had claims for" several things, and the first item on that list is "Weather damage." The same list includes damage from hitting an animal and damage from flying gravel or other flying or falling objects.

Notice what that sentence covers: nonrenewal. TDI's premium tip lists weather claims among the things home companies can't charge you more for. It does not list weather claims under the protections that apply to auto companies. If you are worried about an auto premium after a comprehensive hail claim, ask your company directly how it treats that claim when it renews your policy.

Things that still count, or still matter

Even with those protections, a few points are easy to miss:

  • Discounts and how premiums are figured. TDI's premium tip says you "could also lose any discounts you’re getting for being claim free." Section 551.107 defines a "premium surcharge" narrowly; it says the term "does not include a reduction or elimination of a discount previously received by an insured, reassignment of an insured from one rating tier to another, re-rating an insured, or re-underwriting an insured." If your premium changes at renewal, ask the company in writing what caused it.
  • Everyone's rates can rise. TDI's page on how insurance costs are calculated says companies also consider costs that are "spread across customers," and lists weather events in Texas, including hailstorms, among them, along with inflation and reinsurance. A renewal increase after a big storm season is not necessarily about your claim.
  • Other nonrenewal reasons still apply. TDI lists reasons such as the house being in worse condition than when you bought the policy, the house being vacant for 60 days or more, or the company deciding to limit the coverage it sells in your area. A weather claim does not protect against those.
  • Your claim still goes on your record. TDI's CLUE tip says insurers can report claims to the Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange (CLUE), including the date of loss, loss type, and amount paid, and that a CLUE report shows claims on a house or car for the past seven years. You can get a free copy each year from LexisNexis and dispute wrong information.

Asking a question is not filing a claim

TDI's premium tip says home and auto companies can't charge you more for "Calling your company or agent to ask questions about your policy or the claims filing process." The CLUE tip adds that companies "aren’t supposed to report any questions you ask about your policy or deductible," and advises: "make sure you’re clear about whether you’re filing a claim or just asking a question about your policy."

TDI also says companies can't charge you more for claims they didn't pay, "This includes claims the insurance company denied because your policy doesn’t cover the damage."

If you get a nonrenewal notice

TDI's home and auto nonrenewal tips say your company must give you the reason for a nonrenewal if you ask, and that for policies declined, canceled, or nonrenewed after Jan. 1, 2026, the company must give you a written statement of why. If you think a company nonrenewed or canceled your policy unfairly, TDI says you may file a complaint with TDI. Its help line is 800-252-3439.

The deductible question

None of this tells you whether a particular claim is worth filing. TDI's premium tip suggests finding out your deductible and getting repair estimates first: "If the cost of repairs is about the same or less than your deductible, you may decide it’s not worth filing a claim."
