Hail claims

Should I file if the hail damage is close to my deductible?

Published 2026-11-21

After a hail storm, a lot of people end up with a repair estimate that's a little over, a little under, or right around their deductible, and they wonder whether a claim is worth it. There's no single right answer, but the official guidance points to a few things to check before you decide.

The short version: if the repair cost is about the same as or less than your deductible, the insurer would pay little or nothing, and TDI says you may decide it isn't worth filing. But get a real inspection first, because hail damage is easy to undercount, and in Texas a home weather claim, or a claim the company didn't pay, can't by itself raise your home premium.

Step 1: Know your deductible in dollars

Your deductible is on your declarations page. Our guide to hail coverage and deductibles shows where to look.

  • Car: hail falls under comprehensive coverage, which has its own deductible. TDI's auto guide says you must pay a deductible for collision, comprehensive, and uninsured/underinsured motorist claims.
  • Home: the deductible may be a flat dollar amount or a percentage, and wind/hail may have its own. TDI's deductibles tip says, "If a policy has a deductible that's a percentage, make sure you know how that translates to a dollar amount." Triple-I's deductibles article explains that a percentage deductible is based on the home's insured value: 2 percent on a home insured for $100,000 is $2,000.

TDI's own example shows how much this matters. For two homes insured for $150,000 with $6,500 of hail damage to the roof, the policy with a $500 deductible pays $6,000. The policy with a 5 percent deductible ($7,500) "would not pay anything because the amount of repairs is less than the deductible." These are TDI's illustration numbers, not real prices.

The deductible also applies to each claim, not once a year like many health plans. TDI: "For home and auto policies, the deductible will be applied to each claim."

Step 2: Get a real estimate before you decide

TDI's "Will my premium go up" tip says: "Before you file a claim, find out how much your deductible is... Also get repair estimates. If the cost of repairs is about the same or less than your deductible, you may decide it's not worth filing a claim."

The catch is that hail damage isn't always obvious from the driveway. The NAIC's Post-Disaster Claims Guide says that if the damage is minor, "you might decide you're better off paying for the repairs out of pocket instead of filing an insurance claim. But, remember you might not be able to see all the damage. You may want to have a contractor inspect your home. If you believe the damage will cost more than your deductible to repair, or there's a lot of damage, you may want to file a claim."

Practical tips:

  • Get more than one written estimate. TDI's hail tip says that helps you spot offers that are "too high or too good to be true."
  • For a roof, ask the inspector to check all slopes, vents, gutters, and soft metals, not just what's visible from the ground.
  • For a car, look at the panels in good, angled light, and ask a body shop or dent repair shop for a written estimate.
  • Be wary of anyone who offers to "cover" or waive your deductible. TDI: "It's illegal to waive a homeowners deductible under state law."

Step 3: Know what can and can't count against you in Texas

This is where many people worry most. According to TDI's "Will my premium go up" tip, home and auto companies can't charge you more for:

  • "Claims you file that the company didn't pay." That includes denied claims, and it is relevant if a claim ends up under your deductible.
  • "Calling your company or agent to ask questions about your policy or the claims filing process."

And home companies also can't charge you more for "Claims for damage from natural causes, including weather."

For cars, the rule is narrower. TDI's auto guide says an auto company can't nonrenew your policy because you had claims for weather damage, and that companies use your claims history when setting auto premiums. Our guide Will filing a hail claim raise my rates? goes through both in detail.

Two more things TDI mentions:

  • Claim-free discounts. TDI's tip says your premiums can go up if you file claims and "You could also lose any discounts you're getting for being claim free." Ask your agent how your company treats a paid weather claim for any claim-free discount.
  • Your claims history (CLUE). TDI's CLUE tip says companies can report to CLUE "when you file a claim," including the date, loss type, and amount paid. A filed claim may show up even if little was paid.

Asking a question is not the same as filing

TDI's CLUE tip says: "Companies aren't supposed to report any questions you ask about your policy or deductible. When you're talking to your agent, make sure you're clear about whether you're filing a claim or just asking a question about your policy."

So you can call your agent or company to confirm your deductible and coverage. Just say clearly at the start that you're asking a question, not opening a claim.

Don't wait too long if you might file

If you're unsure, don't let the decision drag on. TDI's home insurance guide says "Most companies have deadlines for you to file a claim. Some policies have a one-year deadline unless you can show good cause for the delay," and that TWIA wind and hail policies have one year from the date of damage. TDI's hail tip says to "File an insurance claim as soon as you can if hail or strong winds damaged your home or car." Check your policy for its deadline.

A simple way to think it through

1. Find your deductible in dollars, for the right coverage (comprehensive for a car, wind/hail or all-perils for a home). 2. Get one or more written repair estimates after a thorough inspection. 3. Estimate clearly below the deductible: the insurer wouldn't pay anything, so filing may not help you. 4. Estimate clearly above the deductible: the NAIC and TDI both suggest a claim may make sense. 5. Close call: remember hidden damage, how your company treats claim-free discounts, and your filing deadline. Ask your agent questions first, clearly as questions.
